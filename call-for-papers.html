Embodied AI and Remote Sensing for Sustainable, Safe Connected Cities (EARS-CONN)

Call for Poster Presentations

EARS-CONN invites research that advances how embodied-AI — from ground robotics to satellite remote sensing — builds a living Digital-Earth for smarter city planning and more resilient urban infrastructure. Submit an abstract or short paper for peer-reviewed poster presentation at EARS-CONN 2027.

Scope

EARS-CONN 2027 is a long-vision initiative on Embodied AI and Remote Sensing for Sustainable, Safe Connected Cities (EARS-CONN). We seek poster presentations that address implementation plans — from embodied-AI agents to robots on the ground — on how they build, support, and protect Digital-Earth through cloud, connectivity, remote sensing, and AI/ML for resilient and sustainable urban ecosystems. A central theme is the application of Digital-Earth to urban insurance intelligence: satellite and aerial data provide the wide-area hazard picture, yet remote sensing alone cannot resolve the property-level detail that dense cities demand. Embodied-AI robotics are a critical complement, capturing roof conditions, structural integrity, micro-topography, and drainage behavior to power hyper-local risk models and property-specific digital twins for proactive underwriting and claims.

Topics of Interest

  • Improving NYC life: robotics and AI for urban quality of life, mobility, safety, sustainability
  • Implementation plans: Digital-Earth and embodied-AI for sustainability assurance
  • Digital-Earth: infrastructure, data fusion, Digital-Earth for urban and environmental applications
  • Data from satellites, robots, drones, autonomous vehicles, UAVs—building and maintaining Digital-Earth
  • Technical, regulation, or assurance perspectives for NYC and urban deployment
  • Urban and environmental sustainability and safety in the NYC area
  • Governance, standards, and monitoring for ethical decisions
  • Urban insurance & risk transfer: Digital-Earth for proactive hazard prediction, property-level risk scoring, digital twins for underwriting and claims, and parametric insurance triggers informed by satellite and robotic data
  • Remote sensing limitations & robotic complement: where satellite/aerial data falls short in urban environments and how ground robotics close the precision gap for insurance, infrastructure assessment, and emergency response

Submission Format

Submit an abstract (1–2 pages) or short paper (2–4 pages) for poster presentation. Accepted submissions will be presented in the poster session during the conference (January 18–19, 2027).

  • Deadline: December 12, 2026 (11:59 p.m. ET)
  • Format: a single PDF in the IEEE two-column conference format; references do not count toward the page limit
  • Content: title, authors and affiliations, problem statement, method or approach, results or expected results, and relevance to EARS-CONN topics
  • Review: each submission is peer reviewed by the program committee
  • Presentation: at least one author of each accepted submission must register for the conference and present the poster
